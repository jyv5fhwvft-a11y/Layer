01Purpose and Objectives

Layer 2 defines the architecture of an individual concept page within the Interactive Knowledge Lexicon (IKL).

Its purpose is to ensure that every concept is taught through a consistent, progressive, accessible and interactive learning experience.

Primary objectives

  • Establish one consistent structure for concept pages.
  • Explain technical ideas from simple to advanced levels.
  • Connect explanations with analogies and practical examples.
  • Support visual learning through diagrams and flowcharts.
  • Provide meaningful interactive learning experiences.
  • Make concept pages reusable and maintainable.
  • Maintain consistent quality across the lexicon.
Core principle: A concept page should not merely define a technical term. It should guide a learner from unfamiliarity to understanding.

02Scope of Layer 2

Layer 2 governs the structure and presentation of individual concept pages. It defines how the learner encounters and progresses through a concept.

Included responsibilities

  • Concept page structure and section order.
  • Content hierarchy and presentation.
  • Visual and interactive learning components.
  • Reusable page-template principles.
  • Navigation within a concept page.
  • Accessibility and responsive presentation.
  • Quality requirements for concept pages.

Outside the scope of this layer

  • Overall learner-experience strategy, governed by Layer 1.
  • Authoritative content storage and registry rules, governed by Layer 3.
  • Shared application functionality, governed by Layer 4.
  • Automated testing and publishing systems, governed by Layer 5.

03Core Concept Page Architecture

Every concept page follows a common architectural pattern. The educational content changes from concept to concept, but the underlying page structure remains consistent.

Concept identity Identifies the subject, category and learning context.
Learning introduction Establishes what the learner is about to understand.
Teaching sections Progressively explain the concept.
Learning activities Help learners explore, practise and test understanding.
Concept connections Connect the subject to the wider knowledge system.
Revision and continuity Reinforce learning and guide the next step.

Architectural consistency

A learner should recognise the same general page organisation when moving from Ping to Process, API, Encryption or another technical concept.

04The Approved 15-Section Teaching Structure

Every concept uses the approved 15-section teaching structure. The depth and examples are adapted to the topic, while the overall learning progression remains consistent.

  1. Concept Identity and Definition
    Introduces the concept, its category and a clear definition.
  2. First-Grader Explanation
    Explains the idea using very simple language.
  3. Analogy and Mental Model
    Connects the unfamiliar concept to a familiar real-world idea.
  4. Beginner Explanation
    Builds a basic but accurate understanding.
  5. Technical Explanation
    Introduces correct technical terminology and deeper detail.
  6. Components and Architecture
    Identifies the important parts and how they relate.
  7. Working Mechanism
    Explains how the concept operates step by step.
  8. Interactive Simulation
    Allows learners to explore or manipulate a simplified model.
  9. Flowchart and Sequence Diagram
    Shows processes, decisions and relationships visually.
  10. Practical Example
    Demonstrates how the concept appears in real situations.
  11. Common Mistakes and Misconceptions
    Corrects misunderstandings and misleading assumptions.
  12. Limitations and Troubleshooting
    Explains boundaries, failure conditions and possible solutions.
  13. Connections to Other Concepts
    Links the topic to related and prerequisite knowledge.
  14. Knowledge Assessment
    Checks whether the learner understands the key ideas.
  15. Quick Revision
    Provides a concise recap of the most important points.
Teaching progression: Simple understanding → mental model → beginner knowledge → technical depth → exploration → application → assessment.

05Standard Page Layout and Visual Hierarchy

The page layout must make the learning journey clear. Learners should be able to identify the concept, understand where they are and find the next section without confusion.

Standard layout elements

  • Concept title and identity.
  • Category and concept identifier.
  • Short introductory explanation.
  • Section navigation or contents list.
  • Clearly separated teaching sections.
  • Visuals and interactive elements within relevant sections.
  • Related concepts and learning navigation.
  • Revision and assessment areas.

Visual hierarchy

Headings, subheadings, paragraphs, lists, diagrams and callouts should have consistent visual roles. Colour and decoration must support understanding rather than distract from it.

06Concept Identity and Navigation

Every concept page must have a recognisable identity and a predictable way for learners to navigate its content.

Concept identity information

  • Concept name.
  • Permanent concept identifier.
  • Category or subject area.
  • Short description.
  • Publication status where appropriate.

Navigation requirements

  • Clear access to the concept's sections.
  • Consistent links back to relevant category or discovery areas.
  • Links to related concepts where relevant.
  • Predictable movement between learning sections.

07Educational Content Presentation

Content must be technically accurate while remaining understandable to learners who are encountering a topic for the first time.

Presentation principles

  • Introduce unfamiliar terms before relying on them.
  • Use short explanations where possible.
  • Explain why a concept matters, not only what it means.
  • Use examples that connect theory to practice.
  • Distinguish simplified explanations from technical precision.
  • Use consistent terminology throughout the page.
  • Adapt the depth of explanation to the concept.
Simplicity must not introduce technical inaccuracies. A beginner-friendly explanation should be an accessible route to the correct technical understanding.

08Diagrams, Flowcharts and Visual Learning

Visuals should explain relationships, structures, sequences and mechanisms that are difficult to understand through text alone.

Suitable visual formats

  • Architecture diagrams.
  • Process flowcharts.
  • Sequence diagrams.
  • Comparison diagrams.
  • Memory and data-structure illustrations.
  • Step-by-step visual demonstrations.

Visual quality requirements

  • Every visual must have a clear teaching purpose.
  • Labels and relationships must be understandable.
  • Diagrams must remain readable on smaller screens.
  • Important information must not exist only in an image.
  • Text alternatives should be provided where needed.

SVG and Mermaid are suitable options for diagrams and flowcharts, subject to the requirements of each visual.

09Interactive Learning and Simulations

Interactive components should help learners investigate how a concept behaves. They should not exist merely as decoration.

Potential interactive elements

  • Step-by-step process demonstrations.
  • Adjustable input and output simulations.
  • Clickable architecture diagrams.
  • Memory or address translation demonstrations.
  • Interactive comparisons.
  • Knowledge-check activities.

Interaction requirements

  • Explain what the learner can do.
  • Make the result of an action understandable.
  • Provide feedback where appropriate.
  • Allow recovery from mistakes.
  • Work with supported touch and keyboard interaction.
  • Do not make essential learning dependent on an interaction.

10Knowledge Assessment and Feedback

Assessment helps learners determine whether they have understood the concept and which areas need further attention.

Assessment approaches

  • Multiple-choice questions.
  • True-or-false questions.
  • Concept identification.
  • Scenario-based questions.
  • Step-ordering activities.
  • Application and reasoning questions.

Feedback principles

  • Explain why an answer is correct or incorrect.
  • Connect feedback to the relevant teaching material.
  • Encourage understanding rather than guessing.
  • Avoid presenting a score as the only measure of learning.

11Progress and Learning Continuity

A concept page should help learners understand their position within the learning journey and identify meaningful next steps.

Learning continuity principles

  • Make the page structure predictable.
  • Provide clear section navigation.
  • Connect prerequisite and related concepts.
  • Support revision and return visits.
  • Connect concept learning to the wider IKL experience.

Any learner-wide progress tracking or persistent records belong to the broader functionality and data layers. This layer defines how a concept page presents and supports learning continuity.

12Reusability and Template Architecture

The concept page must be designed around reusable structures. A new concept should not require the entire page layout to be recreated from the beginning.

Reusable elements

  • Concept identity and introduction.
  • Teaching section presentation.
  • Definition and analogy components.
  • Diagram and simulation containers.
  • Example and callout components.
  • Assessment and revision presentation.
  • Related-concept navigation.

Separation of responsibilities

The reusable template determines how information is presented. The concept's own content determines what information is shown. Shared application functionality is handled by the appropriate application layer.

Reusability principle: One consistent template should support many different concepts without forcing every concept to have identical educational examples or identical interactive components.

13Responsive Design and Accessibility

Concept pages must remain usable across supported screen sizes and input methods.

Responsive requirements

  • Adapt layouts to smaller screens.
  • Keep text readable without unnecessary horizontal scrolling.
  • Ensure diagrams fit the available viewing area.
  • Make interactive controls suitable for touch.
  • Preserve a clear reading order.

Accessibility requirements

  • Use meaningful heading levels.
  • Provide appropriate text alternatives for informative visuals.
  • Maintain sufficient text and control contrast.
  • Make interactive controls identifiable and usable.
  • Do not communicate meaning through colour alone.
  • Support keyboard access where applicable.

14Technical Architecture and Implementation Considerations

Layer 2 establishes the presentation and template requirements. It works with the structured content defined by Layer 3 and the shared functionality defined by Layer 4.

Technology responsibilities

Technology Primary responsibility
HTML Page structure and semantic content areas.
CSS Layout, visual presentation and responsive styling.
JavaScript Rendering, interaction and page behaviour.
JSON Structured concept content and configuration.
SVG / Mermaid Diagrams, flowcharts and visual explanations.

Separation of content and presentation

  • Concept information should be maintained separately from presentation code.
  • The reusable page architecture should interpret structured content.
  • Interactive components should have defined responsibilities.
  • Changes to one concept should not unnecessarily affect others.
The technology choices above describe the approved architectural direction. They do not authorise repository restructuring, migration, file creation or code changes.

15Quality Assurance and Approval Requirements

A concept page should be evaluated for educational quality, technical accuracy, usability and consistency before publication.

Quality checks

  • Correct concept identity and definition.
  • All 15 teaching sections accounted for.
  • Logical progression from simple to technical explanations.
  • Accurate analogies and practical examples.
  • Relevant and readable diagrams.
  • Functional and understandable interactions.
  • Useful assessment and feedback.
  • Working navigation and related-concept links.
  • Responsive presentation and accessibility.
  • Consistent visual identity.

Publication principle

A concept should not be treated as complete merely because its page exists. Its educational content and required functionality must satisfy the relevant quality checks.

17Implementation Boundaries

Approval of this architecture establishes the governing direction for Layer 2. It does not automatically authorise changes to the existing GitHub repository.

Implementation must remain controlled

  • Do not restructure existing files without approval.
  • Do not replace working pages without approval.
  • Do not migrate existing content without approval.
  • Do not introduce a new framework without approval.
  • Preserve working functionality while planning improvements.
  • Explain the purpose and impact of implementation steps.
Important distinction: Architectural approval means the design direction is accepted. Repository changes and implementation actions remain separate decisions.
