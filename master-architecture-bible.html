PART I — MASTER ARCHITECTURE FOUNDATION

1. Executive Overview

The Interactive Knowledge Lexicon (IKL) is an educational platform designed to explain technical concepts and knowledge in a structured, progressive, accessible and interactive manner.

Its purpose is to help learners move beyond memorising definitions and develop genuine understanding.

IKL combines:

  • Clear explanations of technical concepts.
  • Progressive learning from simple to advanced levels.
  • Analogies and practical examples.
  • Diagrams, flowcharts and visual explanations.
  • Interactive simulations and demonstrations.
  • Knowledge assessments and revision.
  • Learning progress and development pathways.
  • Reusable content and shared technical functionality.
  • Quality assurance and controlled publishing.

The platform is designed as an integrated educational ecosystem rather than a collection of disconnected articles.

This Master Architecture Bible consolidates the five architecture layers into one unified reference. It establishes their responsibilities, relationships, boundaries, principles and governance.

2. Vision and Mission

2.1 Vision

To build an interactive knowledge platform that makes complex technical subjects understandable, explorable and practically meaningful for learners at different levels of experience.

2.2 Mission

To transform technical knowledge into structured learning experiences that allow people to:

  1. Discover a concept.
  2. Understand its meaning.
  3. Develop a mental model.
  4. Explore how it works.
  5. Experiment with its behaviour.
  6. Test their understanding.
  7. Connect it with other concepts.
  8. Apply the knowledge in practical situations.
  9. Build demonstrable skills.
  10. Continue learning independently.

2.3 Educational Philosophy

Start simple. Build understanding. Introduce technical depth. Demonstrate practical application. Encourage independent exploration.

A learner should not need advanced technical knowledge before being able to understand the basic purpose of a concept.

3. Core Design Principles

3.1 Understandability

Explanations must be accessible to the intended learning level.

3.2 Progressive Depth

Knowledge should progress from introductory understanding towards advanced technical detail.

3.3 Consistency

Concept pages should follow a common educational structure.

3.4 Reusability

Common functionality, content structures and presentation mechanisms should be reusable.

3.5 Interactivity

Where useful, learners should be able to experiment with concepts rather than only read about them.

3.6 Accuracy

Educational content and technical behaviour must be checked for correctness.

3.7 Accessibility

The platform should accommodate different devices, screen sizes and accessibility requirements.

3.8 Learner-Centred Design

Features should serve meaningful learning objectives rather than exist merely because they are technically possible.

3.9 Controlled Evolution

IKL should evolve through documented, reviewed and authorised changes.

3.10 Creator-Led Learning

The development of IKL should also support the creator's own technical education through the Learn as You Create approach.

PART II — THE FIVE-LAYER ARCHITECTURE

4. Unified Five-Layer Model

The IKL architecture consists of five complementary layers. Each layer has a distinct responsibility. Together, they define the complete platform.

LAYER 1

Learner Experience

Defines what learners experience, how they discover knowledge, how they learn, and how they progress.

LAYER 2

Concept Page and Template

Defines how individual concepts are structured, presented, explained and explored.

LAYER 3

Content and Data

Defines how educational knowledge is organised, represented, stored, related, versioned and supplied to the platform.

LAYER 4

Shared Functionality

Defines the reusable mechanisms and services that support multiple pages and learning experiences.

LAYER 5

Quality, Automation and Publishing

Defines how content, functionality and releases are validated, tested, protected, published and maintained.

4.1 Architecture at a Glance

LayerNameCentral Question
1Learner ExperienceWhat does the learner experience?
2Concept Page and TemplateHow is each concept taught?
3Content and DataHow is knowledge organised?
4Shared FunctionalityHow do reusable features work?
5Quality, Automation and PublishingHow is the platform protected and released?

4.2 Architectural Relationship

The five layers are not five independent products. They are five responsibilities within one platform. The learner experience uses the concept-page architecture. Concept pages consume structured content. Shared functionality supports common learning activities. Quality and publishing controls protect the complete system.

PART III — LAYER 1: LEARNER EXPERIENCE

5. Layer 1 — Learner Experience Architecture

5.1 Purpose

Layer 1 defines the complete learner-facing experience of IKL. It establishes what learners can discover, understand, practise, record and achieve through the platform.

The official Layer 1 reference is the Learner Experience Consolidated Edition 2.0. It consolidates all 126 original approved decisions.

5.2 Learner Experience Domains

Domain 1 — Homepage, Discovery and Navigation

Defines how learners enter the platform, discover subjects, explore categories and navigate between learning resources.

Domain 2 — Reading, Understanding and Accessibility

Defines the reading experience, presentation clarity, comprehension support and accessibility expectations.

Domain 3 — Progressive and Personalised Learning

Defines how learners move through different levels of understanding and receive learning experiences suited to their progress.

Domain 4 — Goals, Learning Records, Portfolios and Skills

Defines how learning objectives, achievements, records, portfolios and skill development are represented.

Domain 5 — Career Development and Employment Readiness

Defines how educational knowledge connects to career preparation and employment development. The consolidated design includes 18 career pathways.

Domain 6 — Interview Preparation and Technical Challenges

Defines interview preparation and technical challenge experiences.

  • Three interview preparation modes.
  • Six evaluation dimensions.
  • STAR-based response support.

Domain 7 — Technical Projects and Practical Experience

Defines how learners connect knowledge with practical projects and demonstrable experience.

Domain 8 — Mentoring

Defines mentoring-related learner experiences and support.

Domain 9 — Learner Support, Help and Community

Defines support mechanisms through which learners can seek assistance and engage with the learning environment.

5.3 Cross-Feature Principles

  • Consistency between learner-facing features.
  • Accessibility and device support.
  • Privacy and security.
  • Clear learning progression.
  • Meaningful learner feedback.
  • Separation of educational objectives from technical implementation.
  • Support for the creator's Learn as You Create journey.

5.4 Architectural Responsibility

Layer 1 specifies the intended learner experience. It does not independently prescribe the complete technical implementation. Layers 2, 3 and 4 provide the structures and mechanisms needed to support it. Layer 5 provides quality controls.

5.5 Preservation Requirement

All 126 approved decisions must remain traceable to the official Layer 1 Edition 2.0 reference. This Master Bible consolidates their domains but does not replace the detailed decision register.

PART IV — LAYER 2: CONCEPT PAGE AND TEMPLATE

6. Layer 2 — Concept Page and Template Architecture

6.1 Purpose

Layer 2 defines the standard architecture for presenting and teaching an individual concept. It establishes a reusable teaching framework so concepts across different technical subjects maintain a consistent educational experience.

6.2 The Concept Page as a Reusable Learning Unit

A concept page should provide:

  • Concept identity.
  • A clear definition.
  • Progressive explanations.
  • A mental model.
  • Technical depth.
  • Visual learning.
  • Practical application.
  • Interactive exploration where appropriate.
  • Knowledge assessment.
  • Revision support.

6.3 The Approved 15-Section Teaching Structure

  1. Concept Identity and Definition — identifies the concept, category and precise meaning.
  2. First-Grader Explanation — introduces the concept in exceptionally simple language.
  3. Analogy and Mental Model — uses familiar comparisons to build a mental picture.
  4. Beginner Explanation — develops the basic explanation using accessible technical language.
  5. Technical Explanation — introduces accurate terminology and deeper technical detail.
  6. Components and Architecture — explains important parts and structural relationships.
  7. Working Mechanism — explains how the concept operates step by step.
  8. Interactive Simulation — allows experimentation where a suitable simulation is possible.
  9. Flowchart and Sequence Diagram — shows processes, relationships and sequences visually.
  10. Practical Example — connects the concept to real situations and practical use.
  11. Common Mistakes and Misconceptions — identifies misunderstandings and clarifies them.
  12. Limitations and Troubleshooting — explains limitations, failure conditions and investigation methods.
  13. Connections to Other Concepts — shows how the concept relates to other knowledge.
  14. Knowledge Assessment — tests whether the learner has understood the material.
  15. Quick Revision — provides a concise summary for reinforcement and recall.

6.4 Standard Presentation Principles

  • Clear headings and subheadings.
  • Consistent visual hierarchy.
  • Readable paragraphs.
  • Well-structured lists.
  • Appropriate diagrams.
  • Clearly separated examples.
  • Interactive elements where beneficial.
  • Responsive layouts.
  • Consistent navigation.

6.5 Reusability

The concept-page template should be reusable. Creating a new concept should primarily involve supplying its educational content and configuration rather than rebuilding the entire page structure.

6.6 Relationship with Layer 3

Layer 2 defines how content is presented. Layer 3 defines how content is represented and organised. Layer 2 consumes the structured content supplied by Layer 3. Generated HTML is a presentation output, not the authoritative content source.

6.7 Relationship with Layer 4

Layer 2 defines where shared functionality is needed. Layer 4 supplies reusable mechanisms such as quizzes, simulations, diagrams and learning-progress services.

6.8 Architectural Responsibility

Layer 2 owns the reusable educational page structure, not the complete storage system or every shared feature implementation.

PART V — LAYER 3: CONTENT AND DATA

7. Layer 3 — Content and Data Architecture

7.1 Purpose

Layer 3 defines how IKL's knowledge is represented, organised, connected, versioned and supplied to the rest of the platform. Its central responsibility is separating educational content from the code that displays or processes it.

7.2 Authoritative Content Principle

Each concept has an authoritative structured JSON representation. The JSON content is the source of truth for the concept's educational information and configuration.

Generated HTML is an output produced from the authoritative content and rendering system. It is not the authoritative content source.

7.3 Central Concept Registry

Each registered concept contains:

  • Permanent concept ID.
  • Concept name.
  • Concept slug.
  • Category.
  • Publication status.
  • Content path.
  • Content version.

7.4 Registry Responsibilities

  • Homepage discovery.
  • Category listings.
  • Search.
  • Concept navigation.
  • Publication-status awareness.
  • Content organisation.
  • Relationship discovery.
  • Monitoring and validation.

7.5 Concept Content Structure

The structured content model includes:

  • Concept identity and metadata.
  • Educational explanations.
  • Analogies and examples.
  • Technical details.
  • Components and mechanisms.
  • Visual resources.
  • Interactive resources.
  • Relationships and prerequisites.
  • Assessment material.
  • Revision content.

7.6 Concept Relationships

  1. Prerequisite — knowledge that should be understood before studying another concept.
  2. Related — another concept connected by subject or meaning.
  3. Contrast — a concept that helps explain differences or comparisons.
  4. Application — a concept that demonstrates where or how knowledge is used.

7.7 Shared Content Resources

Common information should be separated from individual concept files where appropriate:

  • Shared glossary.
  • References.
  • Common examples.

7.8 Versioning

Content version: identifies changes to the educational content of a concept.

Schema version: identifies changes to the structure or format used to represent that content.

These versions must not be treated as interchangeable.

7.9 Publication Status

Concept content must have a publication status. The process must distinguish incomplete, under-review, approved and published material. A concept must not be treated as publication-ready merely because its file exists.

7.10 Validation Requirements

  • Missing content files.
  • Missing required fields.
  • Incomplete teaching sections.
  • Invalid content structures.
  • Broken concept relationships.
  • Incorrect or missing registry information.
  • Inconsistent version information.
  • Publication-status problems.

7.11 Component Configuration

JSON may declare selective component configuration. The relevant rendering or functionality engines interpret that configuration. Content data and the code interpreting it remain separate.

7.12 Architectural Responsibility

Layer 3 owns the organisation and authoritative representation of knowledge. It does not own the visual rendering template or the implementation of shared functionality.

PART VI — LAYER 4: SHARED FUNCTIONALITY

8. Layer 4 — Shared Functionality Architecture

8.1 Purpose

Layer 4 defines reusable mechanisms that support multiple parts of IKL. Its purpose is to prevent every concept page or learning feature from needing its own separate implementation of common functionality.

8.2 Core Architectural Principle

Build shared functionality once, make it reusable, and allow different parts of IKL to use it consistently.

8.3 Shared Navigation and Page Services

Common page services support consistent movement through IKL, including shared navigation behaviour, page-level services and movement between concepts.

8.4 Search and Concept Discovery

The shared search mechanism supports discovery across the concept registry and uses structured concept information supplied by Layer 3.

8.5 Shared Quiz and Assessment Engine

The assessment mechanism supports reusable knowledge checks, consistent question presentation, answer handling and feedback.

8.6 Shared Interactive Simulation Engine

The simulation mechanism supports interactive learning experiences and concept-specific configurations interpreted by a reusable engine where appropriate.

8.7 Shared Diagram and Visual Learning Mechanism

Visual learning services support diagrams and related representations with consistent presentation and reusable mechanisms.

8.8 Shared Calculation and Problem-Solving Mechanisms

Common calculation and problem-solving mechanisms should be reusable where multiple concepts require similar interactions.

8.9 Shared Learning-Progress Mechanism

Progress services support the tracking and presentation of learner progress where such capabilities are implemented.

8.10 Shared Revision and Knowledge Reinforcement

Reusable revision mechanisms support recap, reinforcement and repeated practice.

8.11 Shared Glossary and References

Common glossary and reference services support consistent access to shared learning resources.

8.12 Accessibility and Interaction Support

Shared functionality must account for accessible interaction patterns and usable controls.

8.13 Cross-Feature Communication

Shared services may need to exchange information. Their communication should be clearly defined so that one feature does not depend unnecessarily on the internal implementation of another.

8.14 Modular Components and Independent Failure

Shared components should be modular. A failure in one optional learning feature should not unnecessarily disable unrelated parts of the platform.

8.15 Architectural Responsibility

Layer 4 owns reusable functionality and common mechanisms. It does not own learner-experience decisions, authoritative concept content or publishing approval.

PART VII — LAYER 5: QUALITY, AUTOMATION AND PUBLISHING

9. Layer 5 — Quality, Automation and Publishing Architecture

9.1 Purpose

Layer 5 defines how IKL maintains educational quality, technical reliability and controlled platform evolution. Quality assurance is a continuous responsibility, not a final inspection performed only before publication.

9.2 Educational Content Quality Assurance

  • Factual accuracy.
  • Clarity.
  • Learning-level suitability.
  • Completeness.
  • Consistency with the 15-section structure.
  • Correct use of examples.
  • Appropriate visual explanations.
  • Useful assessment and revision material.
  • Relevant references.

9.3 Technical Quality Assurance

  • HTML structure.
  • CSS behaviour.
  • JavaScript execution.
  • Navigation.
  • Search.
  • Links.
  • Images and resources.
  • Interactive features.
  • Loading behaviour.
  • Responsive presentation.

9.4 Automated Content Validation

Automated validation identifies missing files, metadata, incomplete sections, invalid data, broken relationships, version inconsistencies and publication-status problems.

Automated checks support quality control but do not replace human educational review.

9.5 Automated Functional Testing

Functional testing checks the behaviour of important features, including navigation, search, concept pages, quizzes, simulations, progress mechanisms and shared functionality.

9.6 Regression Testing

Regression testing protects existing working features when changes are made. A new feature or modification is not successful if it silently damages an existing capability.

9.7 Accessibility and Responsive Testing

Quality assurance must check that the platform remains usable across supported devices and accessibility requirements.

9.8 Security and Privacy

Security and privacy checks must be considered before introducing accounts, learner records, integrations, external services or other features involving user information.

9.9 Version Control and Change Management

Changes must be traceable. Important changes should have a documented reason, approval status, test results and release record.

9.10 Backup and Recovery

The platform should preserve important source material and provide a documented approach to recovering from accidental deletion, corruption or unsuccessful changes.

9.11 Controlled Publishing

  1. Prepare the authorised change.
  2. Complete required checks.
  3. Review the publishing candidate.
  4. Publish through the approved method.
  5. Confirm deployment.
  6. Inspect the published result.
  7. Record the release.

9.12 Post-Publication Monitoring

The quality lifecycle continues after deployment. Important pages, links, navigation and interactive features should be checked for problems.

9.13 Error Reporting and Maintenance

Issues should be recorded, prioritised, investigated, corrected, tested and verified.

9.14 Documentation and Release Records

Important architecture decisions, test outcomes, changes, releases and maintenance activities should remain documented.

9.15 Staged Automation Roadmap

STAGE 1 — Basic Validation

Check required files, content fields, completeness and basic data structure.

STAGE 2 — Functional Checks

Check important links, navigation, interactive features and common failures.

STAGE 3 — Publishing Safeguards

Require essential checks before release and protect existing functionality.

STAGE 4 — Monitoring and Maintenance

Monitor published pages, track issues and verify corrections.

STAGE 5 — Continuous Improvement

Review recurring errors and improve validation, testing and publishing processes.

9.16 Human Oversight

Automation assists the quality process. It does not independently replace human judgement in educational accuracy, security, privacy, approval or important publishing decisions.

PART VIII — CROSS-LAYER ARCHITECTURE

10. Responsibility Boundaries

Each layer has a distinct architectural ownership.

ResponsibilityOwning Layer
Learner-facing experienceLayer 1
Concept teaching structureLayer 2
Authoritative educational dataLayer 3
Reusable functionalityLayer 4
Quality and publishing controlsLayer 5

10.1 Boundary Rule

A layer may consume the outputs of another layer without taking ownership of that layer's responsibilities.

  • Layer 2 consumes content from Layer 3.
  • Layer 2 uses shared mechanisms from Layer 4.
  • Layer 1 defines the intended learner experience.
  • Layer 5 validates and protects the outputs of all layers.

10.2 Separation of Concerns

The architecture distinguishes between:

  • What the learner experiences.
  • How educational material is structured.
  • Where authoritative knowledge is maintained.
  • How reusable functionality operates.
  • How quality and publication are controlled.

This separation helps the platform evolve without making every change dependent on every other part.

11. End-to-End Knowledge Lifecycle

  1. Concept Identification: A concept is identified and assigned its identity and category.
  2. Content Preparation: Educational material is prepared using the approved 15-section teaching structure.
  3. Structured Representation: The concept's authoritative information is represented in the Layer 3 content model.
  4. Registry Entry: The concept is registered with its permanent ID, name, slug, category, publication status, content path and content version.
  5. Presentation: The concept-page architecture presents structured content through the reusable Layer 2 template.
  6. Shared Functionality: The page uses relevant Layer 4 services, such as diagrams, assessments or simulations.
  7. Quality Review: Layer 5 checks educational completeness, structural validity and technical behaviour.
  8. Approval: Required human review and publication approval are completed.
  9. Publishing: Authorised content and associated outputs are published through the approved process.
  10. Monitoring and Improvement: The published concept is checked and maintained.
A concept is not complete merely because its content has been written or its page has been generated. It must pass the appropriate quality and publication controls.

12. Concept Data and Rendering Relationship

The architecture establishes a clear distinction between content and presentation.

  • Authoritative content: structured concept data.
  • Content index: central concept registry.
  • Presentation structure: reusable Layer 2 concept-page template.
  • Shared behaviour: Layer 4 functionality.
  • Quality controls: Layer 5 validation and testing.
  • Published output: the learner-facing page.

The content should remain maintainable independently of the code that displays it. Generated HTML should therefore be understood as an output, not the authoritative educational record.

13. Cross-Layer Quality Gates

Gate 1 — Experience Readiness

Does the proposed feature support the intended learner experience?

Gate 2 — Structure Readiness

Does the feature follow the appropriate concept-page and presentation structure?

Gate 3 — Content Readiness

Is the authoritative content complete and structurally valid?

Gate 4 — Functionality Readiness

Do the required shared mechanisms work as intended?

Gate 5 — Quality and Release Readiness

Have the required reviews, tests, approvals and publishing checks been completed?

These gates describe the intended governance model. Their exact technical automation and enforcement mechanisms remain implementation decisions.

14. Error and Dependency Management

The architecture recognises that components may depend on other components. Dependencies should be documented and kept as simple as practical.

Examples include:

  • Concept pages depending on concept data.
  • Search depending on the concept registry.
  • Interactive pages depending on shared engines.
  • Publishing depending on successful validation.
  • Learner-facing features depending on shared navigation.

Where possible, optional features should fail independently. A failure in an optional simulation, for example, should not unnecessarily prevent a learner from reading the concept explanation.

15.4 Accessibility

15.5 Device Support

The platform should support appropriate responsive experiences across devices, including mobile phones and tablets.

16. Privacy, Security and Responsible Design

IKL should adopt responsible practices as its capabilities grow.

  • Collecting only necessary learner information.
  • Explaining data collection and use.
  • Protecting stored information.
  • Reviewing third-party services.
  • Avoiding exposure of credentials.
  • Handling untrusted input safely.
  • Reviewing dependencies and security risks.
  • Providing appropriate user control over personal information.

Any future feature involving accounts, personal records or external integrations must receive appropriate design and implementation review.

17. Content, Schema and Architecture Governance

17.1 Content Change

A change to the educational information of a concept.

17.2 Schema Change

A change to the structure used to represent concept content.

17.3 Architecture Change

A change to the responsibilities, relationships or design of the platform's layers.

These changes have different consequences and should be documented accordingly. A content update should not automatically imply a schema change. A schema change should not automatically imply that the entire platform architecture has changed.

18. Change Approval and Implementation Authority

The Master Architecture Bible defines the intended architecture. It does not grant automatic permission to implement every architectural decision.

The following actions require separate authorisation:

  • Repository restructuring.
  • Renaming or moving existing files.
  • Migrating existing content.
  • Replacing existing page structures.
  • Changing working functionality.
  • Introducing new technical systems.
  • Changing deployment procedures.
  • Introducing data collection or external integrations.

18.1 Existing Project Protection

The existing GitHub Pages project and its working functionality must be treated as protected assets. Architectural planning must not silently become permission to alter the repository.

18.2 Approval Principle

Architecture approval, implementation approval and publication approval are separate decisions.

PART X — IMPLEMENTATION AND EVOLUTION STRATEGY

19. Implementation Philosophy

IKL should evolve through controlled, understandable stages. The platform should not become unnecessarily complicated before its underlying concepts and workflows are understood.

19.1 Learn as You Create

The creator's learning journey is an important part of the project. Development should help the creator understand:

  • The educational purpose of a feature.
  • The architecture behind it.
  • The technology used to create it.
  • How its components work together.
  • How to test it.
  • How to document it.
  • How to improve it.

19.2 Progressive Implementation

Future implementation should proceed in manageable stages. Each stage should have:

  • A defined objective.
  • A clear scope.
  • An explanation of the purpose.
  • A documented change.
  • Appropriate testing.
  • A review point.
  • Explicit approval where required.

19.3 Practical Learning Environment

Instructions for the creator must account for the established working environment: an iPad using Safari and the GitHub website. Guidance should use explicit, small steps and should not assume access to a laptop, desktop, terminal or desktop-only application.

19.4 Preserve Working Functionality

Future development must protect the working homepage, search and existing concept pages unless a specific change has been authorised.

25. Final Architectural Statement

The Interactive Knowledge Lexicon is designed as a unified educational platform built around five complementary architectural responsibilities.

Layer 1 defines the learner experience.

Layer 2 defines how knowledge is taught through reusable concept pages.

Layer 3 defines how knowledge is organised and represented.

Layer 4 provides the shared functionality that supports learning.

Layer 5 protects quality, reliability and controlled publishing.

Together, these layers form the architectural foundation of IKL.

The platform's purpose is not simply to present information. It is to help learners progressively understand, explore, practise, connect and apply knowledge.

The architecture should remain understandable, modular, reusable, accessible and capable of evolving without compromising the existing working platform.

IKL is intended to grow through deliberate learning, controlled building, continuous understanding and documented improvement.
